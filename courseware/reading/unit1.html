Section 2B
DSE 阅读能力等级介绍

Level 3 — General

The main theme or ideas of a paragraph are identified if a text is straightforward. Explicitly expressed views and attitudes are identified.

Level 3 — Specific

Explicitly stated information is understood in fairly complex texts. Straightforward inferences are made. The meaning of words and phrases is identified when a familiar context is given.

Level 4 — General

The main theme or ideas of fairly complex texts are identified. Views and attitudes are identified, and the development of an argument followed.

Level 5 — General

The main theme and sub-themes of complex texts are identified with less familiar themes. Views and attitudes expressed in complex texts are evaluated.

Level 5 — Specific

Inferences are made in a wide range of complex texts. The meanings of words and phrases are identified in any context, including literal and figurative language.

Section 3A
活动引入 Lead-in

📱 Read the message:

Profile: Truman, 35, from the UK; Occupation: construction/trades

Message: "Hi darling! I know we haven't met, but I feel a strong connection with you. My business is having a terrible financial emergency. Could you please transfer $5,000 to help me out?"

Discuss & Brainstorm:
Q1: What are the suspicious details in this message?
❌ Not trustworthy! Multiple red flags: expresses strong emotional connection before meeting; requests $5,000 before meeting; vague "terrible financial emergency"; "construction/trades" is commonly used by scammers; Western male profile targets assumptions about wealth.
Q2: What do we call a person who uses fake stories to cheat people out of their money online?
A scammer (骗子 / 诈骗者). More formal: online scammer / romance scammer / con artist. 区分：scam（骗局本身） vs. scammer（行骗的人）
Q3: Why do people still use dating platforms if romance scams are common?
Four reasons: (1) Loneliness — 都市生活节奏快，难以在线下认识新朋友。(2) Convenience — 手机App随时随地可用。(3) Overconfidence — 大多数人相信"这种事不会发生在我身上"。(4) Social pressure — 尤其对女性（"剩女"标签），家庭和社会期待推动她们使用约会平台。

Jules Hannaford holds her head high as she walks into a Hong Kong coffee shop smiling broadly.

On the first occasion, the Australian allowed a man in the United States to abuse her credit card after she fell for his financial sob stories. The second time, in 2010, proved more costly.

She swallowed the bait after seeing an online dating profile that read: 'Truman, aged 35 from Manchester, United Kingdom. Occupation: construction/trades.'
§7

§11
Although it took time to find the courage to write the book, the experience has been cathartic. Now Hannaford wants it to be a cautionary tale for other lonely hearts who look for dates online.
§12
'Victims usually don't meet the people who scam them. It's all done online with a familiar pattern: "I love you, there's been an emergency, please send money." In my case, everything about Truman, apart from the profile picture, was a lie.'

When her ordeal was over, she learned from police that Truman was a career criminal with more than 20 aliases and a rap sheet dating back to the 1990s. She had lost a significant amount of money, she says.

§15
She advises people to meet 'online lovers' in person in their own country, have video chats and be extra careful with money transfers. Her advice might sound obvious, but increasing numbers are falling prey to dating scams, Hongkongers included.

Section I covers Hannaford's personal story — how she was scammed twice. Key terms: sob stories, bait, due diligence, red flags, cautionary tale.

Section 3B
Text 1: Statistics & Response

Section II · §16-23

§16
In the first half of this year, 272 online dating and romance scam cases were reported in the city, up 2.5 times from the same period in 2017, according to the Anti-Deception Coordination Centre under the Hong Kong Police's Commercial Crime Bureau.
§17
This year the centre has coordinated 23 operations, arresting 30 suspected scammers and preventing the remittance of more than HK$500 million.
§18
In November 2017, Hong Kong and Malaysian police arrested a Kuala Lumpur-based syndicate which allegedly duped 48 Hong Kong women and one elderly man out of HK$29.5 million. In 2016, the police released a video based on a real-life case, hoping to raise awareness about romance scams.
§19
Hannaford is also on a mission to raise awareness. Last month she spoke at an event hosted by networking community Metta, attended by 100 people — 70 per cent of them women — and she has shared her story on 'Hong Kong Confidential', a podcast series she set up in 2017.
§20
Politicians have spoken out on the subject. Last month, Secretary for Security John Lee Ka-chiu urged the public to be vigilant about dating scams, while Sham Shui Po district councillor Ramon Yuen Hoi-man is supporting victims of a new scam trend in which people are tricked into buying 'birthday gifts' costing between HK$1,000 and HK$6,000.
§21
'In these cases scammers buy cheap gifts on [Chinese shopping site] Taobao and then expect the recipient to buy more expensive gifts in return,' Yuen says.
§22
Hong Kong-based dating and relationship coach Valentina Tudose says internet dating scams are a painful reality for many women — and smart professional women are falling prey. Police figures show that women account for 95 per cent of victims, and they generally possess higher academic qualifications, are computer-literate and able to communicate in English.
§23
'Don't think what happened to Jules could never happen to you,' says Tudose.

Section II provides statistics, police actions, and expert opinions. Key data: 272 cases, 95% female victims, HK$500M prevented.

Section III-IV · §24-30 + Comments

§24 (III)
Hong Kong's dating landscape is tough to navigate. People have little free time due to long working hours, and a gender imbalance, with 921 males for every 1,000 females, according to the Census and Statistics Department.
§25
Cultural factors also come into play with family pressure to marry and avoid being a 'leftover woman' — a derogatory term for unmarried women in their late twenties and beyond.
§26
For example, Hongkonger Ginny Lim — not her real name — is under family pressure to find a husband. She is 32, has a good job in digital marketing, but has never had a serious boyfriend. She has used dating sites but is wary and believes pressure to get married is a reason why so many women fall for romance scams.
§27 (IV)
In the age of dating apps and socially acceptable e-stalking, a new lexicon has evolved. One expression is catfishing, in which scammers befriend victims through online social platforms, especially dating apps such as Tinder, using fake identities and profiles, and trick them into thinking they are somebody else, as in Hannaford's case.
§28
Another is ghosting, when someone ends a relationship by disappearing suddenly without explanation, withdrawing from all communication. Hong Kong-based psychotherapist Gabrielle Tuscher is seeing a growing number of young women, some still in their teens, fall victim to ghosting.
§29
'Young girls on dating apps like Tinder and Coffee Meets Bagel think they've met these wonderful guys, correspond for weeks, book a date and then the guys don't show up and never respond. They just vanish,' says Tuscher.
§30
'The impact that can have on a young girl's self-esteem is devastating.'

★★★ Q2

What does "due diligence" (line 13) imply?

Hannaford did not verify/check Truman's background before trusting him. She took him at face value. "Due diligence" (商业术语:尽职调查) = she made no basic checks on his identity. §8: "red flags she failed to notice" — these red flags are what due diligence would have discovered.
🔑 Inference 策略：定位原句 → 关注并列/对比结构 → 结合上下文验证 → 用自己的话 paraphrase。

★★★ Q3 — True / False / Not Given (4 marks)

According to paragraphs 5-8, are the following statements True (T), False (F) or Not Given (NG)?

#StatementT / F / NG
i)Hannaford did not sympathize with scammers' stories of financial hardship.
TFNG
ii)Online scams left Hannaford bankrupt.
TFNG
iii)Hannaford accepts some blame for being scammed.
TFNG
iv)The signs of a scam should have been apparent to Hannaford.

(i) F — §5: "she fell for his financial sob stories" = she DID sympathize with his stories of hardship.
(ii) NG — §5-8 只说她 "lost a significant amount of money"（§13），并未提到 bankrupt（破产）。
(iii) T — §7: "I took Truman at face value without doing due diligence" = 她承认自己有责任。
(iv) T — §8: "red flags she failed to notice or...refused to notice even though they were fluttering in front of her" = 危险信号本应显而易见。
🔑 TFNG 三判据：T = 原文直接支持；F = 原文直接矛盾；NG = 原文没提（不要脑补！"很多钱" ≠ "破产"）。

★★★ Q4

Find the metaphor Hannaford uses to describe the signs of a scam in paragraphs 7-8.

Red flags (字面=红旗, 比喻义=危险信号/警告标志). §8: "red flags...fluttering in front of her" — 红旗就在眼前飘扬，她却视而不见。Metaphor 判断关键 = 字面义 ≠ 实际义。此隐喻来自赛车/裁判用语——红旗=停止/危险。

1. Demographic reality: 女性多于男性 → 客观上找伴侣更难.
2. Cultural expectation: 社会反而给女性更大结婚压力（"剩女"标签）.

Section 4
Global Reading（宏观阅读）

Global Reading 是一种自上而下的阅读方式。在处理文章细节之前，先退一步问自己三个问题：

  1. 这是一篇什么类型的文章？
  2. 作者写这篇文章的目的是什么？
  3. 文章怎么组织的——每个部分在做什么？

如何略读 (Skimming)

• 标题 Title — 表示文章的主题
• 引言段 — 引入话题或者设定场景
• 各段主题句 Topic sentences — 通常为首句
• 结尾段 — 通常展示作者立场

Global Reading 对应的三类 DSE 题型

1. Alternative Title — 理解全文主旨 (Q18)
2. Writer's Purpose / Intention — 理解写作目的 (Q19)
3. Section Heading Matching — 理解各部分主题与结构 (Q20)

Section 4A-4B
Alternative Title & Writer's Purpose

Alternative Title 三条件

☑ Accurate（准确） — 标题内容是否有实际依据？
☑ Comprehensive（全面） — 是否覆盖所有主要部分？
☑ Specific（具体） — 是否专属这篇文章？

排除法

✗ 只描述某一部分 → 太窄
✗ 可套用于任何同类文章 → 太广
✗ 引入未涉及的信息 → 不准确

Writer's Purpose 判断步骤

目的动词文章信号
to inform中立语气；呈现事实，无明显立场
to warn警示性语言；提示潜在风险或危险
to advise具体可行的建议；引导读者采取行动
to criticise对某现象或做法提出负面评价
to entertain故事性强；趣味优先，结论次要

🔑 核心陷阱： "to describe X" ≠ "to warn about X" ≠ "to advise on X" — 目的动词的选择决定整道题的方向。

Section 4C
Section Heading Matching 三步法

Step 1 — Skim 首尾

阅读每个 section 的 第一句 + 最后一句，两句定主题方向。

Step 2 — 思考段落主旨/功能

思考 1：寻找段落主旨
• 这个文段中是否存在 topic sentence？
• 这个文段谈论得最多的中心内容是什么？
• 这个文段中的话题是否有方向性转变？

思考 2：这个 section 承担什么角色？

常见功能英文表达
引入背景/人物Introducing the context
提出问题/现象Presenting the issue
提供证据/数据Supporting with evidence
分析原因/影响Analysing causes or effects
给出建议/结论Drawing conclusions

Step 3 — 排除确认

确认一个匹配后立即划掉该选项，防止重复使用。🔑 核心陷阱： 只描述 section 内某一段的 heading 是错的 — heading 必须能代表整个 section。

Liam's father developed Alzheimer's — it was heart-breaking. He started reading about memory-training techniques.
§5
Liam acknowledges we outsource memory to technology: alarms, map apps, calendar alerts. "A strong memory makes us wiser. It's useful in your career, in exams and in relationships."
§6
Draw pictures (physical or mental) — the more bizarre the image, the better it sticks in your memory.
§7
Create a memory palace — imagine walking through a familiar place and placing items you want to remember in different rooms.
§8
Use word association — e.g. "Robin Whitely": think of the bird Robin, depressed because red faded to white. The more colourful and unusual the image, the more memorable.
§9
Remove all technology for an hour daily. Go out for a walk without your phone. Pay attention to your surroundings without digital distractions.
§10
Rest your brain by doing nothing at all — an experiment showed participants recalled 49% of information after resting, compared to just 14% after completing a different task.
§11
Take a nap — sleep cements memories. Getting enough rest is one of the simplest and most effective ways to strengthen recall.
