💡 Reading skill 1: Identifying the main idea 识别主旨

阅读文章时，我们通常先识别主旨——即文章或段落中最重要的思想或观点。识别主旨有助于理解文章内容并进行概括。当主旨被直接表达时，通常出现在主题句（topic sentence）中，多位于段首或段尾。段落其余部分是支撑或解释主旨的信息。

When we read a text, we often start by identifying the main idea, which is the most important thought or opinion in a text or paragraph. It is useful to identify main ideas because they help us understand what a text is about and allow us to make summaries. When the main idea is directly expressed, it is often found in a topic sentence, usually at the beginning or end of a paragraph. The rest of the paragraph contains information that supports or explains the main idea.

Skills builder 技巧训练
  • 先读段落的首句和尾句
  • 快速扫读段落其余部分，识别支撑信息
  • 若首句或尾句概括了主旨，即为主题句；否则通读全段，自行推断主旨
⚠️ 注意并非所有段落都有主题句。此时需要自行概括段落来推断主旨。记住：举例或提供支撑信息的句子不是主题句。
A1 · Read the following paragraphs and take note of the main ideas and supporting information.
§1
In my opinion, it's much better to binge-watch a whole series of a TV programme rather than watch one episode a week. When you binge-watch, you watch all the episodes one after another. This means that you can become completely immersed in the programme, like watching a really long film. When you watch just one episode a week, you forget what has happened and the themes of the show are harder to follow.
§2
In a recent survey, many people said that watching films at home was more comfortable, more convenient and cheaper than visiting the cinema. As TVs have increased in size, the main advantage of the cinema—a large screen—has been lost. With attendance down, cinema managers are trying to lure customers back with special offers and improved food options. However, more and more people prefer to watch films at home instead of going to the cinema.

🔍 主旨 vs 支撑信息

§1 · Main idea

Main idea（主题句·首句）: It's much better to binge-watch a whole series of a TV programme.
Supporting info: become completely immersed; one episode a week → forget what has happened, themes harder to follow.

§2 · Main idea

Main idea（主题句·尾句）: More and more people prefer to watch films at home instead of going to the cinema.
Supporting info: survey results (comfortable / convenient / cheaper); bigger TVs; cinemas' special offers.

🔁 Reading skill 2: Paraphrasing and summarizing paragraphs 改写与概括

前面学到段落主旨可以在主题句中找到。然而，并非所有段落都有主题句。此时需要改写（paraphrase）和概括（summarize）段落来描述主旨。改写是用不同的词语表达原文含义；概括是提炼出要点。

You learnt that the main idea of a paragraph can be stated in a topic sentence. However, paragraphs do not always have a topic sentence. In this case, you need to paraphrase and summarize the paragraph to describe the main idea. Paraphrasing means expressing what someone has said or written using different words. Summarizing means giving a statement of the main points.

To summarize a paragraph 概括段落的步骤：
  • 仔细阅读段落，理解作者意图
  • 忽略支撑信息和例子，聚焦段落整体大意
  • 用自己的话概括主旨——改写原文，语言简洁明了
📝 策略提示练习概括能帮助你应对考试中"用一句话概括主旨"的题型。

Text 1: Avatar: a sci-fi masterpiece（§1–§6）
§1
Released in 2009, Avatar is a spectacular science-fiction blockbuster which remained the highest-grossing film for a long ten years. With this film, director James Cameron once again reinvented the genre in dazzling style.
§2
The setting is 22nd-century Pandora, a moon in the Alpha Centauri star system. Humans want to mine an important mineral, but the Na'vi, aliens that live on Pandora, feel threatened. Jake Sully, a soldier, connects his mind to a Na'vi body to control it. This way, he can move around in Pandora's poisonous atmosphere. While Sully is supposed to fight the Na'vi, he falls in love with one of them and decides to support their cause.
§3
What stays with you long after the credits have rolled is Pandora itself. Cameron truly managed to create a new world, one with completely original plants, animals and landscapes. Floating islands of rock soar above misty jungles, while glowing carnivorous plants snap at bizarre insects. The Na'vi themselves are also captivating and beautiful. These tall, blue, long-tailed aliens move realistically, and the detailed expressions on their faces convey emotions as well as any human's. Within minutes, you forget that they are computer-generated.
§4
Not everything in Avatar is so enchanting, however. The dialogue, as in many of Cameron's films, is clichéd and humourless. The acting can seem exaggerated. The film's score is loud and intrusive in the usual Hollywood manner. The themes—war and capitalism are bad, the environment and love are good—are hardly fresh. Characters are either angelic or evil.
§5
Still, Avatar is a remarkable achievement and one of the few films to actually change cinematic history. The film brought 3D technology into the mainstream for the first time and showed audiences that CGI films weren't just for children. Cameron's obsessive attention to detail shows in every frame.
§6
While more of a technical success than a classic story, Avatar is the most important sci-fi film since Star Wars. The four upcoming sequels will no doubt make it into a classic franchise too. Avatar may not evoke many deep thoughts or feelings, but few films are so imaginative and capable of transporting you to another time and place. For that reason, everyone should watch Avatar at least once.

Text 2: James Cameron（§1–§6）
§1
Hollywood may be known for the oversized egos and extreme behaviour of its stars, but even there James Cameron stands out. A risk-taker and workaholic, Cameron has written and directed some of the most expensive and successful films in history, including The Terminator, Titanic and Avatar.
§2
James Francis Cameron was born in Canada in 1954 and moved to the USA in 1971. After seeing Star Wars in 1977, he decided to become a film-maker. He started off working in visual effects while writing screenplays in his spare time. Cameron was given the chance to direct his first film in 1981, but was fired after two weeks for reasons unknown.
§3
His next film, The Terminator (1984), had a far better outcome. It became a commercial and critical hit, and made Cameron famous. A string of other successes followed: Aliens (1986), The Abyss (1989) and Terminator 2: Judgement Day (1991). These sci-fi films were all notable for their groundbreaking visual effects, an element of film-making that Cameron has moved forward significantly.
§4
While his obsessive personality motivated Cameron to create ever more impressive cinematic spectacles, it earned him a fearsome reputation in Hollywood. The Abyss, much like the later Titanic (1997), was plagued with financial and practical problems. Cameron reacted to the setbacks by piling pressure onto the actors and crew, shouting until he got his way. Over the years, the long hours and on-set stresses have taken a toll on Cameron's personal life, resulting in four divorces.
§5
Titanic's difficulties were forgotten when it became the highest-grossing film ever and won 11 Academy Awards. Cameron received three of these and celebrated in typical style, shouting, 'I'm the king of the world!' In fact, Titanic was inspired by Cameron's passion for the deep sea, which he has visited many times in submarines to make documentaries about sunken ships. He is also the first person to visit the Challenger Deep, the deepest point in the world's oceans, alone.
§6
In 2009, Cameron released Avatar to positive reviews and massive financial success. The film's themes showed a different side to Cameron, a long-time vegan and environmentalist. With many film projects on the horizon, Cameron has no plans to reduce his heavy workload.

Text 3: Superhero films: going strong or soon to be gone?（§1–§6）
§1
As I passed my local cinema today, I noticed that half the film posters featured brave-looking, muscular actors in colourful skintight suits: superheroes. Whichever cinema you go to in most countries, it's almost guaranteed that there will be at least one superhero film playing. This is the age of the superhero, the most popular genre around.
§2
However, many people are starting to question how long this popularity will last. One of them is director Steven Spielberg, known for Jaws and Jurassic Park. He recently talked about how westerns enjoyed similar success but died out in the 1970s. 'Will superhero films,' he asked, 'soon go the way of the western?'
§3
There are reasons to think that the answer is 'yes'. The fact is that studios are running out of comic book characters to adapt for the big screen. The best-known ones such as Batman and Spider-Man have been heavily used, while others—such as the Fantastic Four—have failed to catch on, despite multiple attempts. Lesser superheroes such as Aquaman may cause cinema-goers to shrug their shoulders.
§4
Given that most superhero films have the same basic storyline—with the main character battling evil forces and winning—film-makers try to bring something new to each release. Usually, this means going bigger and longer, with multiple villains, more action and extended running times. But this is unsustainable. When superhero films already cost US$150–356 million to make and last for over two hours, the bigger and longer approach can't continue forever.
§5
Superhero films have been so successful partly because they tap an existing market. Many superhero fans grew up reading comic books and were eager to see their favourite stories on the silver screen. However, these groups are getting older and younger generations may not share their enthusiasm, being more at home with computer games and social media. Changing audience tastes are a clear threat to profits.
§6
Despite these challenges, superhero films aren't on their way out yet. They are reliable money-spinners, and huge markets like China are still hungry for the genre. It's worth remembering that westerns were the top draw for five decades, and the superhero genre is nowhere near that old. The key to survival will be variety: female-centred superhero films like Captain Marvel and more realistic, drama-based releases like Logan will provide the change in direction that the genre needs.
Text 4: WONDER WOMAN/CONAN #1（§1–§6）
§1
Comic book crossovers, where one character appears in the world of another, are the latest trend. They can allow readers to learn more about the characters, but they can also be a lazy attempt to cash in. Fortunately, this six-issue miniseries from writer Gail Simone and artist Aaron Lopresti, a crossover between Diana of Themyscira and Conan of Cimmeria, is breathtaking.
§2
Wonder Woman was created as a feminist response to macho superheroes. She is a clever and amazingly fast warrior. Conan is also a warrior, but he lived thousands of years earlier. Unlike Wonder Woman, he has no superpowers, just a sword, muscles and a love of fighting. Despite having very different characters and being from different eras, they make a great team.
§3
In this first issue of the crossover, Conan agrees to protect a gambler. In order to pay him, the gambler takes Conan to the city of Shamar. Here, the cruel Dellos forces his slaves to fight before he sells them. One of these slaves is Wonder Woman, who wins fights against a number of men. Dellos captures Conan and sets up a fight between the two heroes.
§4
While only 32 pages long, the comic is quite slow to get started. There are explanations of both characters' backgrounds, which take up many pages. Once the two characters have met, the action speeds up, leading to the gripping cliffhanger ending. The plot is generally driven by the characters, rather than external events.
§5
Lopresti's artwork is full of drama, especially in the fight scenes, and there is plenty of detail. We can see the expressions of every character, however minor, and the settings are lifelike and inviting. The artwork is nothing less than magnificent.
§6
Lopresti's drawing skills and Simone's talent for dialogue have created a fascinating comic that will please both Conan's and Wonder Woman's fans. Despite the unlikely nature of the crossover, it works very well and readers will not be disappointed.

Unequal fortunes of the film industry（§1–§6）
§1
Fortunes in the film industry have shifted dramatically over the past 20 years. While mainland China's film industry has grown markedly, Hong Kong's influence has waned. What does the future hold for these two markets?
§2
When the Chinese-made film Monster Hunt was launched in 2015, no one could have predicted it would become a blockbuster. The comedy animation/live-action film, which cost 350 million yuan to make, became the highest-grossing Chinese film ever and earned more at the box office in a single day than any other Chinese film. The figure is all the more impressive when you consider that, on average, only 15 per cent of Chinese films are profitable.
§3
Sceptics argue that the film had an unfair advantage because its release was set for July, the month when the Chinese government bans the release of foreign movies in order to protect domestic films. But others point out that Chinese film companies are increasingly coming up with new and innovative business models that are turning the industry on its head and are the source of such new-found success.
§4
Tencent, Asia's second-biggest Internet company, provided financial backing for the film and helped to promote it. It sees a future where the film-making process will operate in reverse; namely, marketing teams find out what viewers want to see, and this forms the basis of the film the company makes. The pioneer plans to use big data, online ticket sales and social media to get a more accurate picture of what viewers want. This is a radical departure from the usual business model, where viewers are spoon-fed movies that have been approved by company executives in a boardroom.
§5
The future certainly looks rosy for the Chinese film industry, which is now the second largest film market after the USA. Box office takings have grown 36 per cent since 2013, and the country is building 5,000 new cinema screens every year. With such optimistic belief in the movie industry, it is not surprising that big businesses are keen to invest, and not just in the mainland market. One such player is Alibaba Pictures Group, a subsidiary of Alibaba, the biggest Internet company in China, which recently went into business with Paramount Pictures to promote the latest film in the Mission: Impossible franchise. Now China's movers and shakers are looking beyond their shores to Hollywood.
§6
As mainland China's film industry has flourished, Hong Kong's film industry has withered. Back in the 1980s, Hong Kong films were respected around the world. This was Hong Kong's golden era, when it was considered as a leading player, behind only Hollywood and Bollywood. Hong Kong films such as A Chinese Ghost Story and A Better Tomorrow were hugely popular across Southeast Asia, and films made in Hong Kong regularly won awards at international film festivals. Sadly, the last time a Hong Kong film was shown at an international festival was in 2009, when Vengeance was screened at the Cannes Film Festival. In the mid-1990s, Hong Kong was making more than 200 films a year. By 2000, this number had fallen to less than 70. So what went wrong?
Unequal fortunes of the film industry（§7–§11）
§7
The first problem was over-production: it is possible to have too much of a good thing. As hundreds of movies were churned out each year, production values fell, storylines became clichéd and the industry focused more on production quantity, rather than quality. Then a wave of piracy hit takings at the box office and DVD sales. The Asian financial crisis was the final nail in the coffin for many film-makers.
§8
Politics have also played a role. When Hong Kong returned to China in 1997, filmmakers began looking to the north, where the riches lay. Hong Kong's studios, producers and directors were only too aware that the mainland market was burgeoning, and they wanted a piece of the action. In order to tap into this lucrative market, film-makers began collaborating with partners on the mainland. The money-spinners came thick and fast, but something else was lost in the process.
§9
Film-makers catered to mainland tastes, rather than the local tastes in Hong Kong. The films were also subject to stricter censorship regulations, with topics such as gambling, crime and superstition being off-limits. In the race to boost their bottom line, some film-makers have been accused of selling out, of forgetting their roots and abandoning their creativity. The end result is a local film industry that has lost its identity, as the local market and traditions were all but forgotten.
§10
Thankfully, attempts are now being made to revive the Hong Kong film industry. The Hong Kong government established the Film Development Fund in 2007 in a bid to help new film-makers, and competitions such as the Fresh Wave short film competition are encouraging a new generation of film-makers to tell their stories. These initiatives are already starting to bear fruit as up-and-coming talents produce films such as Still Human, Trivisa and Mad World that address local issues without thinking too much about the potential market or costs.
§11
China's movie industry juggernaut will continue to charge ahead and Hong Kong's film industry will probably never be able to compete with it in terms of scale. But the new breed of films emerging from Hong Kong show that they can succeed by following a different route: telling thoughtful, honest stories that resonate with audiences around the world. In the end, Hong Kong's film industry may be like the phoenix that rises from the ashes, and all the stronger for the struggle it has endured.

✍️ Writing skills: Supporting opinions with reasons, facts and examples 用理由、事实与例子支撑观点

当我们表达观点时，可以用理由、事实和例子来支撑，使观点更加有力。它们不仅向读者解释了我们持此观点的原因，还能使论点更有说服力。我们应至少为每个观点给出一个理由，并用例子加以佐证。例子是说明论点正确性的事实，应当切题、简短，且为读者所熟悉或易于理解。

When we give an opinion about something, we can support it with reasons, facts and examples to make it more solid. Not only do they show the reader why we hold our opinions but they can also make our point of view more convincing. We should try to give at least one reason for each opinion and back it up with an example. Examples are facts that illustrate why the argument is right. They should be relevant, fairly short and either familiar to the reader or easy to understand.

Example 范例
Unfortunately, the plot and characterization are sometimes inconsistent. For instance, Wonder Woman is supposed to be a pacifist but she solves her problems with violence. She fights in nearly every action scene. In addition, she is a feminist icon, but cannot succeed without help from men.
  • 给出理由（reason）支撑观点
  • 添加例子（example）佐证理由——例子应当客观真实
  • 再用同样格式给出另一个理由，进一步支撑观点

❓ Rhetorical question（反问）

在写作中提出问题却不期望回答，而是为了强调观点或引发思考，这种问题叫反问句（rhetorical question）。

When we ask a question in our writing not because we expect an answer but because we want to make a point or provoke thoughts, it is called a rhetorical question.

Example from p. 140: In China, Sherlock fan fiction is all the rage. In Korea, the K-pop band SHINee recorded a tribute song. With this much love, why wouldn't directors continue to produce shows featuring our favourite detective? The original detective is still the best.

反问句特别适用于议论文和演讲稿，也可出现在其他文体中。作用包括：

  • 强调观点，吸引读者注意 — 如 How could we allow this cruelty to continue?（我们怎能允许这种残忍继续下去？）
  • 以不那么直接的方式表达立场 — 如 Wouldn't it be better to allow ordinary film fans to vote for the Oscars?（让普通影迷投票奥斯卡不是更好吗？）
  • 引发思考 — 如 Can we pretend that climate change is fake?（我们能假装气候变化是假的吗？）

🔂 Parallelism（平行结构）

平行结构是一种重复手法——重复的是特定的语法结构（而非单个词），且在文中紧密出现。

Parallelism is a form of repetition in which a particular grammatical structure (rather than a single word or phrase) is repeated close together in a piece of writing.

Example from p. 142: The film's score is loud and intrusive in the usual Hollywood manner. The themes—war and capitalism are bad, the environment and love are good—are hardly fresh. Characters are either angelic or evil.

平行结构特别适用于议论文和演讲稿，也可用于其他文体。功能包括：

  • 表明两个动作或事物之间的联系 — 如 The more you watch this actress, the more you will recognize her tremendous talent.（越看这位女演员的戏，越能认识到她的巨大才华。）
  • 让文章更易记 — 如 I loved how the film started, but I absolutely hated how it ended.（我喜欢电影的开头，但极度讨厌它的结局。）
  • 赋予文章节奏感与流畅度 — 如 She is respected for her talent, for her award-winning performances and for her gracious behaviour towards her co-stars.（她因才华、获奖表演和对同事的优雅举止而受人尊敬。）

🔗 Unit 4 Writing skill builder What are relative clauses?

定语从句（relative clause）是句子中用来指明某个人或物、或补充更多信息的部分。

A relative clause is the part of a sentence that tells us which person or thing is being talked about, or gives more information about a person or a thing.

定语从句分为两类：限定性（defining）和非限定性（non-defining）。两类均可由关系代词（who, whom, which）以及 whose, when, where, why 和 that 引导。

There are two kinds of relative clauses: defining and non-defining. Both kinds can be introduced by relative pronouns (who, whom, which), as well as whose, when, where, why and that.

Defining 限定性

提供必要信息，指明"哪一个"——去掉后句意不完整。不加逗号。

Non-defining 非限定性

提供补充信息——去掉后句意仍完整。前后加逗号。

🔗 Defining relative clauses 限定性定语从句 · About people

限定性定语从句包含必要信息，明确指出我们所谈论的人或物（即哪个名词）。

Defining clauses contain essential information that clearly indicates which person or thing (i.e. which noun) we are talking about.

About people 指人

Relative clause with 关系词Examples 例句
who/that as the subject
作主语		The old couple who/that live next door seldom have visitors.
住在隔壁的那对老夫妇很少有待客。
who/whom/that as the object
作宾语		The boy who/whom/that we met just now is Susan's brother.
我们刚才碰到的那个男孩是 Susan 的弟弟。
whose + noun as the subject
whose + 名词作主语		I have a friend whose brother is a famous designer.
我有一个朋友，他哥哥是著名设计师。
📌 注意1. 关系代词作宾语时可以省略。 2. 口语中，作宾语时更常用 who 而非 whom。 3. 口语中常用 that 代替 who。
1. As the object, the relative pronoun can be omitted. 2. Informally, who is more common than whom as object. 3. That is often used instead of who in informal speech.
🔗 Defining relative clauses About things/places · where/when/why

About things/places 指物/地点

Relative clause with 关系词Examples 例句
which/that as the subject or object
作主语或宾语		Did you see the book which/that was on the table this morning?
你看到今早桌上那本书了吗？
📌 注意1. 作宾语时关系代词或 that 可省略。 2. 指物的限定性定语从句也可用 whose，但通常用 of which 或 with 短语替代。

With where, when and why 用 where/when/why

可以在表示地点、时间、原因的名词后用 where, when 和 why 引导定语从句。有时 that 可替代 when 或 why。

We can use where, when and why to introduce relative clauses after nouns that refer to place, time and reason. Sometimes that is used to replace when or why.

About 指代Examples 例句
whereplaces 地点The hotel where we stayed was quite far from the airport.
我们住的那家酒店离机场很远。
whentime 时间Do you remember the day when we first met?
你还记得我们第一次见面的那天吗？
why/thatreasons 原因Is there a reason why/that you don't like her?
你有什么不喜欢她的理由吗？
📌 注意口语中 when/that 或 why/that 可以省略。

Infinitives and participles 不定式与分词替代定语从句

  • 在 first, second, next, last, only 及最高级之后，to-infinitive（不定式）比定语从句更常用。
    例：Who was the last person to leave the classroom yesterday?
    昨天最后一个离开教室的人是谁？
  • 当从句动词表示持续或重复动作时，可用现在分词（present participle）。
    例：People living in tropical countries usually wear lightweight clothing.
    生活在热带国家的人通常穿轻便衣物。
🔗 Non-defining clauses 非限定性定语从句

非限定性定语从句不指明人或物，而是为已明确的人或物提供补充信息。

Non-defining clauses do not tell us which person or thing is being described. We use these clauses to provide additional information about a person or thing that has already been specified.

例：My father, who works in a bank, often comes home late for dinner.（我爸爸在银行工作，经常晚归。）

位于句中的非限定性定语从句用逗号与句子其余部分隔开。若从句在句末，则在引导词（who, which 等）前加逗号。

With who, whom, which

About 指代Subject/Object 主/宾Example(s) 例句
whopeople 人subject 主语I'm grateful to Paul, who helped us when we moved house.
我很感激 Paul，他帮我们搬家。
whompeople 人object 宾语It was not easy to break up with Paul, whom she had known for years. (formal 正式)
和认识多年的 Paul 分手并不容易。
whichthings 物subject or object 主/宾We visited the Great Wall, which was magnificent.
我们游览了长城，非常壮观。

With whose, where and when

About 指代Example 例句
whosepossession 所属 人/物Paul, whose sister is my classmate, is leaving for Australia tomorrow.
Paul 明天要去澳洲，他妹妹是我同学。
whereplaces 地点We took minibus number 20R to Bride's Pool Waterfall, where we had a barbecue.
我们坐 20R 小巴到新娘潭瀑布，在那里烧烤。
whentime 时间That September, when we met, was one of the wettest on record.
我们相识的那个九月是有记录以来最多雨的之一。

With which (referring to a whole sentence 指代整个句子)

which 可以指代整个句子，而非仅指前面的名词。

例：Susan failed the test, which surprised us all.（Susan 考试不及格，这让我们都很惊讶。）

📌 注意1. 非限定性从句中，who, whom, which 不能用 that 替代，也不能省略。 2. 口语中常用 who 代替 whom。
🔗 Prepositions + which and whom 介词 + 关系代词

在限定性和非限定性定语从句中，都可以在 which 和 whom 前使用介词（如 for whom, in which 等）。

We can use a preposition before which and whom (for whom, in which, etc.) in both defining and non-defining relative clauses.

例：The girl about whom I told you is here now. (formal)（我跟你提过的那个女孩现在在这里。）

📌 注意1. 非限定性从句中可用 some of/one of/three of/each of/many of/much of/all of/none of 等结构 + whom/which。
例：Jane has two brothers, both of whom are married.（Jane 有两个哥哥，都已婚。）

2. In informal situations 口语中：

  • 限定性从句中，介词通常放在从句末尾，并用 that 代替 which 或 who/whom。
  • 非限定性从句中，介词可放在从句末尾，用 who 代替 whom，但不能用 that，也不能省略关系代词。
✍️ 写作要点有时可用定语从句来衔接或延伸故事中的想法。
例：I met Tom, who told me that Ann had returned to Hong Kong.（我遇见了 Tom，他告诉我 Ann 已经回到香港了。）

🔄 The passive voice 被动语态

主动句（active）中，主语是动作执行者，即施事者（agent）。
被动句（passive）中，主语是受动作影响的人或物。（可用介词 by 引出施事者，如 The burglars were arrested by two police officers。）

In an active sentence, the subject is the person/thing that does the action, i.e. the agent. In a passive sentence, the subject is the person/thing affected by the action. (We can use by to identify the agent.)

We use the passive voice 被动语态的用法：

Usage 用法Example(s) 例句
when the action is more important than the agent
动作比施事者更重要		Apparently, an old Japanese bomb has been discovered in Victoria Park.
显然在维多利亚公园发现了一枚日本旧炸弹。
when it is obvious who the agent is
施事者显而易见		The burglars were arrested late last night.
窃贼昨晚深夜被捕。
when we do not know who the agent is
不知道施事者是谁		The school has been vandalized again.
学校又被破坏了。
when it might be insensitive to name the agent
指出施事者不礼貌		It's clear that some mistakes were made.
显然出了一些差错。
with modal verbs
与情态动词连用		Every precaution must be taken. The concert might be cancelled because of the rain.
必须采取一切预防措施。音乐会可能因雨取消。
in that-clauses with the subject it
it 引导的 that 从句		It is believed that the new rules will be effective.
人们相信新规将会有效。
✍️ 写作要点被动语态常用于报告（如调查报告）、学术写作和科学写作——这些文体需要客观、非个人化的风格。
⚠️ 注意1. 不及物动词不能用于被动语态。 2. have, fit, lack, resemble, suit 等动词表示状态而非动作，虽为及物动词但不能用被动。 3. 某些主动动词可有被动含义，如 open, read, sell, wash, write 等。
