Section 3 · Brainstorming

🧠 3. 想法点 Brainstorming

💡思考3.1: 我们在第一课中学习过评估一个措施是否可行，主要从哪四个方面展开？
可行性、效果、副作用、必要性/有替代方案。
💡思考3.2: 你认为从法律层面界定"highly dangerous"是否可行？
较难。也有人在玩跑酷这类极限运动时安全无患，也有人在滑雪、拳击时不小心受重伤。所以是否危险也取决于个人技术等其他因素。要从法律层面界定"highly dangerous"就很复杂。
💡思考3.3: 若highly dangerous sports真的被界定为违法了，你认为是否所有人就会因此而止步这类活动？
不一定。那些热爱极限运动的人其实本来就是较少数，这些人可能还是会在违法边缘试探擅自开展这类活动且未被发现。
💡思考3.4: 界定highly dangerous sports违法，是否会带来其他副作用？
可能会侵害个人自由。有些人认为极限运动有培养信心和韧性的积极作用，且本身有较强的安全意识和技术。对这些人来说可能会侵害他们的选择自由。
💡思考3.5: 为了保障公民安全，除了界定highly dangerous sports违法，是否有其他方案？
有。如加强安全教育、场地隐患排查、普及安全道具等。

Section 6 · PEER

📐 ⭐ Task 6B: 根据PEER论证公式，在句首横线上填入每句话的功能。

论证公式 PEER

符号英文问题含义
P Point What is your point? 表明观点
E Explain/explanation Why / How? 展开解释（为什么 / 如何）

📝 范文

Task: Write an argumentative essay discussing whether highly dangerous sports should be made illegal. Support your arguments with reasons.

In recent years, several accidents involving extreme sports have led to serious injuries and even deaths in Hong Kong. As a result, some people believe that highly dangerous sports should be made illegal. While this idea may seem reasonable (at first glance), I believe banning such sports would be an unwise decision because it is impractical, ineffective and may create negative consequences.

To begin with, making highly dangerous sports illegal would be difficult to carry out in practice. One major problem is that it is not easy to clearly define what counts as "highly dangerous". While sports such as parkour or rock climbing may appear risky, many other activities, such as skiing, boxing or horse riding, also involve a certain level of danger, making it difficult to decide where the line should be drawn. In addition, enforcing such a law would require a large amount of manpower and monitoring. Therefore, banning dangerous sports may simply be unrealistic and difficult to enforce.

Even if the policy could be introduced, it would still be unlikely to solve the problem effectively. Some people may continue participating in dangerous sports secretly. Without professional supervision or proper safety guidance, this may make accidents even more serious. More importantly, accidents are often caused by many different factors rather than the sport itself. Some people may underestimate risks, ignore safety rules or fail to prepare properly. In other cases, accidents may be caused by bad weather or difficult terrain. Therefore, making such sports illegal may not effectively reduce injuries or deaths.

Another major concern is that banning dangerous sports may unfairly limit personal freedom. Adults should have the right to make their own choices, especially when they fully understand the risks involved. Many people take part in extreme sports not simply for excitement, but also to challenge themselves, build confidence and develop perseverance. Therefore, the government should not unnecessarily interfere with personal choices simply because certain activities involve risks.

Some people may argue that because several people have died while taking part in extreme sports, the government should ban these activities in order to protect public safety. While protecting lives is certainly important, this argument overlooks the fact that banning sports is an overly extreme solution to a more complex problem. Accidents are often caused by multiple factors rather than the activity itself. Instead of introducing strict bans, the government should focus more on improving safety education, strengthening supervision and encouraging the use of proper protective equipment. These measures would be more practical and effective in reducing accidents while still respecting individual freedom.

In conclusion, although banning highly dangerous sports may seem like a good way to improve public safety, it would be difficult to enforce, unlikely to solve the problem effectively and unfair to individual freedom. Rather than introducing unnecessary restrictions, the government should adopt more practical and effective ways to reduce risks (while still respecting personal freedom.)
