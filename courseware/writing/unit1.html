📝 Body 1 — Feasibility

PEER 论证结构 + 连排问句

阅读以下句子，判断每句话在段落中的作用，点击"作用："后的空白查看答案 →

To begin with, the proposal is impractical and difficult to carry out. （作用：______）
Even if some schools are willing to share their sports facilities, many problems would arise. （作用：______）
Who would be allowed to use the facilities? How would schedules be arranged? "What rules would be introduced?"
（作用：______）
These are difficult issues that many schools are not prepared to handle. （作用：______）
As a result, expecting schools to organise public access to sports facilities may simply be unrealistic. （作用：______）

论证公式 PEER

要素含义说明
P (Point)What is your point?表现观点
E (Explanation)Why / How?展开解释（为什么/如何）
E (Example)How / what?举例/列举/具体化
R (Result)So what? Why it matters?结果/影响/最终判断

🌟 Notes: PEER 只是一个论证参考公式，并不代表每一段都必须完整使用 PEER 的所有部分。只要论证链条完整，能够清楚解释观点并提供足够支持即可。

思考7.2: 三个问句的作用？

本段连续使用三个问句（Who? How? What?），如果改成陈述句版本，语气会更强还是更弱？为什么？
连排问句可以加强语气，让问题看起来更多、更复杂，也更难解决。相比之下，陈述句则平淡、较客观。连续问句还能引导读者思考，因此会让论证更有说服力。这种写法特别适合 letter to the editor，因为它可以让作者的立场更明显、语气更有力度。

📝 Model Answer

📝 范文：Letter to the Editor

Task: Write a letter to the editor of the Hong Kong Daily stating your opinion on opening school sports facilities to the public. (~400 words, 6 paragraphs)

Dear editor,

I am writing in response to recent suggestions that schools should open their sports facilities to the public after school hours because there are not enough sports venues in the community. While this idea may sound reasonable, I believe it is ultimately unwise for several important reasons.

To begin with, the proposal is impractical and difficult to carry out. Even if some schools were willing to share their sports facilities, many problems would arise. Who would be allowed to use the facilities? How would schedules be arranged? What rules would be introduced? These are difficult issues that many schools are not prepared to handle. As a result, expecting schools to organize public access to sports facilities may simply be unrealistic.

Even if the plan could be carried out, it would still be unlikely to achieve its intended goal of solving the shortage of sports facilities in the community. School sports facilities are mainly designed for students and usually include facilities such as basketball courts, swimming pools and table tennis tables. However, community members of different ages may have a wider range of needs, such as badminton courts, jogging tracks or fitness facilities, which some schools may not have. In addition, school facilities are often limited in size and may not be able to accommodate large numbers of users. Therefore, even if schools opened their facilities, the proposal would still be unlikely to fully meet the public's needs.

Finally, opening school facilities to the public may create several problems for both schools and students. Sports equipment must be checked and maintained regularly. With more people using the facilities, damage may happen more quickly, leading to higher maintenance costs. Schools may also need to hire extra staff such as security guards or cleaners, which would increase expenses. Moreover, opening school facilities to the public may put student safety at risk. For example, basketball courts may become damaged after frequent use, and uneven ground or broken basketball hoops may increase the risk of accidents. This means students are more likely to get injured during PE lessons or sports training.

Some people may argue that school sports facilities are often unused after school hours, so opening them to the public would make better use of resources. However, this argument overlooks the fact that it is not schools' responsibility to provide sports facilities for the public. School facilities are mainly designed for students, not for public use. If there is a shortage of sports facilities in the community, it is the government's responsibility to solve the problem instead of placing the burden on schools.

In conclusion, although the proposal may be well-intentioned, it is impractical, unlikely to fully meet public needs, and may negatively affect schools and students. Instead of expecting schools to solve a wider social problem, the government should take responsibility and invest in proper community sports facilities to better meet public needs.

Yours faithfully,
Chris Wong
